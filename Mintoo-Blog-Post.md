# The Big Question for Coders: AI, Exponential Growth or Complete Demise?

*I used AI to help create this title. But how does it change the way you code?*

You're working on a programming project and you’ve now spent 30 minutes staring at the same error. God, it's getting annoying. You change a few lines, recheck your notes, run the program again, and somehow make the error worse. YOU GIVE UP. open ChatGPT immediately and get your solution in seconds.

As a computer science student, I've faced this situation more times than I'd like to admit. I know more friends and peers do the same too! The speed pulls you in. AI could help you learn faster, debug faster, and understand concepts faster. The problem starts when you care more about working code than understanding your code.

## AI Isn't the Problem

AI already plays a major role in programming. Students entering software development need to know how to work with AI tools. Used well, AI gives you a tutor whenever you need one. You could ask for another explanation of recursion, trace a sorting algorithm step by step, compare two solutions, or get feedback on code you already wrote.

This gives students a huge learning advantage. Instead of spending an hour searching through lecture notes because you misunderstood one concept, you could ask a focused question and spend that same hour learning the why and how.

The better question is: **How do you use AI without letting AI do the learning for you?**

[Research on students using large language models in programming education](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2026.1814773/full) examines how students use LLMs while learning programming. Students ask these tools to explain concepts, debug errors, and help write code. The research also raises concerns about students relying on LLMs instead of building their own programming skills.

Consider two students stuck on the same infinite loop.

One asks:

> Why does my loop never terminate? Do not give me the corrected code. Help me find what I am doing wrong.

The other asks:

> Fix my code.

The difference matters.

## When Help Becomes a Crutch

Programming requires more than writing code. You need to break down problems, test solutions, read errors, trace variables, debug code, and explain your choices. Both students above could submit the same working program, but they took different paths to reach the answer. The first student still needs to find the bug and fix the code. The second student gets the answer.

Suppose you spend 40 minutes debugging a function. You read the error, print variable values, trace each line, test your assumptions, and eventually find the bug. ChatGPT might find the same bug in 15 seconds. Those extra minutes still gave you something useful. You practiced debugging.

If you send every error to AI as soon as you get stuck, you skip this practice. Your assignments might work and your grades might look good but then someone asks you to solve a new problem without AI??

## A Different Story: The Technical Interview

For instance, in a technical interview for an internship. The interviewer gives you a programming problem and asks you to explain your solution.

- Why did you choose this data structure?
- What is the time complexity?
- What happens with an empty input?
- How would you improve your solution?

Reading dozens of AI-generated solutions will not help if you never practiced building and explaining solutions yourself. AI also makes mistakes. ChatGPT might give you slow, broken, or incorrect code. You need enough programming knowledge to spot those problems.

If AI writes your code, explains your code, and checks your code, ask yourself how much programming you did yourself.

## A Better Way to Use AI

I follow a simple four-step guideline:

**Attempt → Ask → Understand → Rebuild**

1. **Attempt:** Start the problem yourself. Read the error, test your ideas, and find the point where you get stuck.

2. **Ask:** Once you find the problem, write a clear prompt. In an Accenture workshop, I learned the **R-E-A-C-H** prompt engineering framework: Role, Examples, Action, Context, and Hone. Instead of writing, *"Fix my code,"* give AI enough context to help you learn. 

   > I am a CS student learning linked lists in Java. Here are a few examples we’ve done in class. My remove() method fails when I remove the final node. I think my problem involves updating tail. Act as a tutor and not give me the corrected code. Give me hints one at a time.

   **Prompt AI to guide your thinking, not replace it.**

1. **Understand:** Do not stop when your program runs. Trace the code. Check your variables. Explain each decision. You could even turn AI into a quiz tool: *"Ask me three questions about this solution. Wait for my answers before explaining anything."*

2. **Rebuild:** Close the AI response and solve the problem again. Recreate the reasoning yourself. If you get stuck, review the concept and try again.

## Think First, Prompt Second

AI gives you two choices. Use it to skip the struggle OR use it to make the struggle more productive!

Ask for explanations and hints. Ask AI to quiz you. Compare approaches and challenge its answers.

The next time ChatGPT fixes your program, do not move straight to the next assignment.

Ask yourself:

> **Could I solve this problem again on my own?**
